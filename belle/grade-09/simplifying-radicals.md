# Simplifying Radical Expressions

We want to simplify:

$2\sqrt{32} + 3\sqrt{50} - 3\sqrt{18}$

---

### Step 1: Factor each number inside the square root

Look for **perfect squares** you can pull out (like $4, 9, 16, 25, 36$, etc.).

$2\sqrt{16 \times 2} + 3\sqrt{25 \times 2} - 3\sqrt{9 \times 2}$

---

### Step 2: Rewrite each perfect square as a square number

$2\sqrt{4^2 \times 2} + 3\sqrt{5^2 \times 2} - 3\sqrt{3^2 \times 2}$

---

### Step 3: Pull the squares out from under the square root

(When you take the square root of $a^2$, you just get $a$.)

$(2 \times 4)\sqrt{2} + (3 \times 5)\sqrt{2} - (3 \times 3)\sqrt{2}$

---

### Step 4: Simplify each term

$8\sqrt{2} + 15\sqrt{2} - 9\sqrt{2}$

---

### Step 5: Combine like terms

They all have the same radical part ($\sqrt{2}$), so add or subtract the numbers in front:

$(8 + 15 - 9)\sqrt{2} = 14\sqrt{2}$

---

**Final Answer:**

$\boxed{14\sqrt{2}}$

---

**Tip:**  
When you see a square root, always check whether the number inside has a perfect
square factor. You can think of it like “pulling out” the square factors and
leaving the leftovers inside.
